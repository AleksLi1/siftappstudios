About

For most of history the future was left to the gods. Peter Bernstein’s Against the Gods tells how people learned to measure risk and then to trade it. The word comes from the early Italian risicare, to dare. In his words, “risk is a choice rather than a fate.”

Markets depend on people who make that choice. An airline buys its fuel a year ahead, and an exporter sells dollars it has not yet been paid. Each gives up a little to know its costs, and whoever takes the other side is paid for carrying the uncertainty.

Prices also stray from what is known. A trader who buys what is too cheap and sells what is too dear is paid for the correction, and the price is more accurate for everyone who trades after.

Aphelion Capital takes these risks with its own money, in liquid markets. Every position is sized so that no single loss can decide the firm’s future.
